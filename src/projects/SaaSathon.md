# Beyond the Code: Lessons from Judging and mentoring the Inaugural SaaSathon
![SaaSathon](https://www.saasathon.dev/opengraph-image.png?opengraph-image.14hr0z0~h9cff.png)

The lights at the University of Canterbury didn’t really go out all of this past weekend. When you pit a group of hungry, talented students against a 48-hour clock and a $10,000 NZD prize pool, sleep becomes a secondary concern. This past weekend was the first-ever **SaaSathon**, and I had the privilege of stepping out from behind my own projects to serve as a mentor and judge.

## The Challenge: From Idea to MVP
The brief was deceptively simple: build a productivity-focused SaaS application that uses Artificial Intelligence in a creative and useful way. But as anyone in engineering knows, "simple" is often the hardest thing to execute. 

The transition from a Friday night pitch to a Sunday afternoon functional demo is a brutal gauntlet. What I witnessed was nothing short of incredible. We didn't just see "wrappers" around LLMs; we saw teams identifying genuine friction in how people work and building tools to solve it. Seeing the technical grit required to handle API integrations, front-end polish, and prompt engineering under such time pressure was a testament to the caliber of talent currently at UC.

![Me On The Judging Panel](src/projects/SaaSathon/one.jpg) *Me on the judging panel*

## The Internal Seesaw: A Second-Year Perspective
Stepping into a position of authority at this stage in my degree was, frankly, a surreal experience. As a second-year student, I found myself sitting across the table from teams that often included people my own age or older. This created a peculiar psychological tension that I didn't expect.

I spent much of the weekend swinging between two extremes:
1. **Imposter Syndrome:** The voice in the back of my head asking, *"Who are you to tell them their architecture is flawed?"*
2. **The Ego Surge:** Whenever a piece of advice I gave led to a "Eureka" moment for a team, I’ll admit I felt a surge of self-importance that was likely a bit over-inflated. Plus someone thought giving me a lanyard that said staff wa a good idea and didn't really help with this issue.

## The Pivot to Business Viability
The most profound lesson I learned was how to ground that emotional volatility by shifting my perspective. I realized that my value as a mentor wasn't just in evaluating the elegance of the code, but in assessing the project as a whole. 

I forced myself to move beyond the technical challenge and ask the "non-technical" questions:
* **Market Fit:** Is this actually a viable business, or just a cool feature?
* **Unit Economics:** How does the cost of compute (token usage) scale with the user base?
* **Defensibility:** What is the "moat"—why couldn't a competitor build this in a week? Also can you app be replaced by a claudecode skill?

This shift—viewing a project through the lens of sustainability rather than just execution—was the bridge that helped me move past my own insecurities. It reminded me that in the real world, **the tech is the engine, but the strategy is the steering wheel.** Focusing on whether a product *should* exist helped me provide better value than just focusing on how it was built.

## The Judging: 300 Seconds to Win
The final hours of the SaaSathon were defined by a relentless pace: each team had exactly five minutes to pitch their entire weekend’s work. As a judge, this was the most challenging part of the event. We weren’t just looking for a cool demo; we were looking for clarity of vision, technical feasibility, and that elusive "business moat".  
The quality across the board was so high that separating the teams was a matter of razor-thin margins. While every project showed immense promise, the top three podium spots eventually went to:
### First Place: ATLAS
An incredible tool designed to accelerate the tedious building consent process using AI to navigate regulatory hurdles.
@[ATLAS](https://atlas-construction-web.vercel.app) *ATLAS Website*  
### Second Place: Howards Real Estate
A high-utility product that allows real estate agents to transform a simple set of photos into professional, narrated video walkthroughs automatically.  
![Howards Real Estate](src/projects/SaaSathon/four.jpg) *Howards Real Estate Pitching*

### Third Place: MOAD
A project management platform specifically for contract developers. It uses AI to bridge the communication gap with non-technical clients, keeping them informed while effectively axing scope creep before it starts and keeps both parties in the loop about costs. 

@[MOAD](https://monad-weld.vercel.app) *MOAD Website*

### Innovation Beyond the Podium
Beyond the winners, several other projects deserve a massive shoutout for their creativity and technical ambition.I was particularly moved by the team that stepped outside the purely software-focused SaaS mold to develop a hardware solution aimed at increasing typing speeds for individuals with paraplegia. 

In a similar vein of industry-specific innovation, another team built a tool for fashion designers that could take a raw sketch and instantly estimate a Bill of Materials, even allowing designers to shop around for fabrics based on the AI’s analysis.  

I cannot stress enough how difficult the final deliberations were. All the projects were genuinely impressive, and the judging discussion reminded me that the "right" answer  often isn't about who has the best code, but who can best articulate a solution to a painful, real-world problem
